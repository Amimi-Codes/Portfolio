Chapter 01 · The Quest Begins

Everyone wants to play D&D. Fewer survive making a character.

Almost everyone I know has said "I've always wanted to try D&D" at some point. Then they open the Player's Handbook. Twelve classes, a pile of species, subclasses, backgrounds, ability scores, saving throws, proficiencies, spell slots… and that's all before you've rolled a single die.

Beginners don't struggle because the game is boring. They get stuck because every choice seems to need three other rules to understand it, and it's hard to tell which ones matter right now.

The rulebook way

  • Proficiency bonus
  • Hit dice
  • Cantrips
  • Saving throws
  • Subclass
  • Spell slots
  • Alignment
  • Ability modifiers
  • Background
  • Feats
  • Armor class
  • Sub-species
  • Point buy?
  • Darkvision
  • Skill proficiencies

The BG3 way

  1. Pick who you are. Species and class shown, not described.
  2. See what it means. Only the details that matter at that step.
  3. Start playing. The rest unlocks as you go.

The inspiration

Then Baldur's Gate 3 happened.

BG3 runs on D&D 5e rules, yet friends who had never touched a d20 spent hours in its character creator, and they were having fun. It showed every option visually, explained things only when you needed them, and let you watch your character take shape as you made each choice.

That's what I wanted for tabletop: the same depth, with a path through it that doesn't require reading 300 pages first.

Chapter 03 · Tales by the Fire

Every hero has an origin story. This one has three.

  1. Version 1 · ~2023

    The hard-coded era

    It started almost three years ago, before 5.5e was even born. Every species, class and stat was typed out by hand, and the design had the raw charm of a level 1 commoner: it worked, and that's about all it did.

  2. Version 2 · Mimi's D&D Character Builder

    Rolling with 5e and BG3

    A rebuild around D&D 5e and Baldur's Gate 3 rules: species and sub-species, class selection and a character summary for planning a level 1 hero. Built to be a trusty companion for "our favourite dating sim / dress-up / role-playing game."
