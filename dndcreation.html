Chapter 01 · You find yourself in a tavern

Many want to play D&D. Few come out of the character creation process unscathed

Almost everyone I know has said "I've always wanted to try D&D" at some point. And then they open the Player's Handbook. Twelve base classes, a pile of species (and sub-species because who plays as a human right?), subclasses, backgrounds, ability scores, saving throws, proficiencies, spell slots… and that's all before you've rolled a single die.

Beginners don't struggle because the game is boring. They get stuck because every choice seems to need three other rules to understand it, and it's hard to tell which ones actually matter.

The rulebook way

  • Proficiency bonus
  • Hit dice
  • Cantrips
  • Saving throws
  • Subclass
  • Spell slots
  • Alignment
  • Ability modifiers
  • Background
  • Feats
  • Armor class
  • Sub-species
  • What is point buy?
  • Darkvision
  • Skill proficiencies

The BG3 way

  1. Pick who you are. Species and class shown, not described.
  2. See what it means. Only the details that matter at that step.
  3. Start playing. The rest unlocks as you go.

The inspiration

The BG3 approach

Many players are all too familiar with staring at a blank character creation sheet or the first step of creating a character on D&D Beyond, and feeling overwhelmed. Baldur's Gate 3 (BG3) has a different approach: it shows you the options visually, explains them only when you need them, and lets you watch your character take shape as you make each choice.

That's what I wanted for tabletop: the same depth, with a path through it that doesn't require reading 300 pages across 4 different books first.

Chapter 02 · Scouting Report

What adventurers told me

I've been gathering feedback from people who play D&D and people who have wanted to but never quite finished the character creation process, about where character creation loses them.

  • 4 survey responses so far
  • 3 of 4 had played only once or twice
  • 2 of 4 stopped or put off creating their character
  • 3.25 / 5 average confidence playing the finished character

Early read: the survey is still open, so treat these as signals, not statistics.

Finding 01 · The boss isn't where you'd think

Picking a class and species was the easy part

Nobody found choosing a class or species hard. The trouble starts afterwards, with the list-heavy, mechanical steps. When asked what tripped them up most recently, three of four people named spells, equipment and reading the rules.

Finding 02 · Party members wanted

The best guide was a person at the table

All four found a friend or another player helpful, and two named a friend as their single most useful source of help. Everyone who tried a pre-made example character found it helpful too. Video tutorials helped nobody.

"Having someone actually there tailoring advice to your situation is the best way to learn hands-on."

Finding 03 · Lost in the woods

Half of them stalled

One person never finished their character and another came back to it later. The complaints were time and maths, and that includes BG3: its one player spent four hours in the character creator.

"There's so much math…"
"There's too many customization options that I spent 4 hours making my character instead of playing the actual game."

Finding 04 · Nobody asked for an edition

Beginners want to know what a choice does

Only one person (the Dungeon Master) had an edition preference; the others didn't care or didn't know what an edition was. What they asked for was explanation: "actual instructions", and what a choice means once the game starts.

"Specific explanations on how choices could affect gameplay, such as what spells are useful in which scenarios."

Loot · What this means for Origin

  • Spend the help where it hurts. Keep class and species quick; put guidance and recommended picks into skills, spells and equipment.
  • Be the friend at the table. Short, in-context answers to "what does this do?" instead of rule text.
  • Start from an example. Offer ready-made characters people can tweak instead of a blank sheet.
  • Show the payoff. Explain how each choice plays out in a game, and do the maths for them.
  • Don't lead with the edition. Default to current rules and keep the setting out of the way.

Chapter 03 · Tales by the Fire

Every adventurer has a backstory. This one has three.

  1. Version 1 · ~2023

    The birth of Origin

    It started almost three years ago, before 5.5e was even born. Every species, class and stat was typed out by hand, and the design had the raw charm of a level 1 npc: it barely had any depth and had the same repeat dialogue for every species and class. And thus began the tale of Origin.

  2. Version 2 · Mimi's D&D Character Builder

    Character Development Arc

    Origin stayed stagnant and didn't make it out of the tavern... Then Baldur's Gate 3 came along. This project which had been collecting dust was starting to see movement. Our favourite "dating sim" game was now a beginners character creation tool, and it was inspiring me to dust Origin off and give it a second chance.

  3. Version 3 · In progress

    The Quest Begins

    A revamp is in the works, aiming to bring Origin up to speed with the latest D&D rules and BG3-inspired features. Levels were added, graphics were improved, and the user interface was streamlined.
